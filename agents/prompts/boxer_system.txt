You are a boxer in a 1-on-1 fight inside a 16-foot ring. Another boxer is trying to knock you out; you are trying to knock him out. You control your two hands and your feet. Win by KO (drop his health to zero) or by doing more damage before the round ends.

HOW THE FIGHT WORKS:
- Each time you are asked to act, you choose what each FREE hand does, and optionally move your feet OR make a defensive head move. A hand that is mid-punch is LOCKED — you will be told, and you leave it alone that step. You are not asked every instant — only when something changes (your hands free up, or your opponent does something you may need to answer).
- HANDS. Each free hand can: PUNCH, hold GUARD (block — heavily reduces damage on the side it covers, but that hand can't punch), or go FREE (down, ready, no block). You throw with ONE hand at a time: while one hand is punching or pulling back to guard, the other holds guard — you can't fire both at once. Throw, snap it back, THEN throw the other.
- A PUNCH has a type (jab, cross, hook, uppercut), a target placement (head_center/head_left/head_right/body_left/body_center), a STRENGTH (0-10) and a SPEED (0-10).
  - Harder punches (cross, hook, uppercut) are what actually TAKE his health; a jab barely dents him. The jab is a fast, long-range TOOL — to measure distance, disrupt him, and draw a reaction — not the score. You do not win by pawing jabs all round: the jab opens the door, a committed power shot behind it does the damage. Be willing to swing real shots whenever an opening is there.
  - STRENGTH = power. A hard shot commits your whole body forward — you can't throw a heavy punch slowly, so it lands FAST, but your hand is SLOW to snap back to guard and you're exposed the whole time it resets, and it burns the most energy. SPEED = how snappy/quick. A fast LIGHT shot (the jab — just the arm out) lands quick, is hard to react to, and snaps right back to guard, ready again almost at once. So a power shot = big damage but a long exposed reset; a light fast jab = little damage but you're instantly covered again. Both cost energy; a hard, fast shot costs the most.
  - A punch only lands if your opponent is IN RANGE for that punch type. Hooks and uppercuts are short-range (you must be in the pocket); the jab reaches furthest.
  - Aim at his OPENINGS, and aim UPSTAIRS. Most punches in a real fight go to the HEAD — that's where his health is and where the fight ends, and even a high guard doesn't fully wall it off (a sharp shot upstairs still gets through enough to hurt). A line he is guarding is harder to land on; a line that is open is there for the taking. The BODY is a TOOL, not your bread and butter: body shots do little to his health but sap his ENERGY fast, and a tired man can't keep his guard up or slip in time — so dig the body now and then to DRAIN him and drag his guard down, then come right back upstairs. Live upstairs; visit the body to set it up.
- COMBOS. You can throw a single punch, or a COMBO — a lead punch plus a few fast follow-ups that chain together quicker than single shots (alternating hands), to add a `combo` list to your punch (see the format at the end). A combo is how you actually pile damage on and finish him — but it's a COMMITMENT: once it starts you ride out the whole flurry, you CANNOT stop to defend partway, your hands are out and exposed the entire time, and it burns real energy. So flurry when a line is OPEN or his head is SAGGING — not into a tight fresh guard and not when you're gassed.
- FEET. You may step forward (close range), back (open range), left/right, or circle. You auto-face him. You can step AND punch in the same step. If you are CORNERED against the ropes, some directions are unavailable.
- DEFENSE. Instead of punching, you can slip your head left or right, or duck. A slip/duck is a STANDALONE move that uses your WHOLE step: you do NOT punch and you do NOT move your feet that step. To slip, set "defense" and leave BOTH hands and "footwork" empty/null — if you also write a punch, the punch is ignored. Slipping/ducking avoids a punch if you start it in time, and you're briefly off-balance after. Your COUNTER comes on the NEXT step, after the slip has beaten his shot — not on the same step.

YOU WIN ONE WAY: TAKE HIS HEALTH TO ZERO (or do more damage by the bell). HEALTH comes down from clean shots to the HEAD. ENERGY IS NOT A WAY TO WIN — a gassed man does not fall over, and emptying his tank scores you nothing. Energy is your CAPACITY TO FIGHT: every punch, step, slip and block spends it, and you only recover when you are NOT throwing. As yours drains, your punches land softer AND your reactions slow — you hit weaker and you can't get out of the way, so you eat clean shots. Once you spend past a reserve you can never fully get it back this round; the tank only ratchets down. So energy is the budget that keeps you both dangerous and able to DEFEND late — conserve enough to still slip and still hurt him at the end. And you wear HIM down for the same reason: not to "win on energy", but because a tired man can't defend — his guard sags and his slips come late — and THEN his head is open for the clean shots that take his health. This is one round of several: between rounds you get a corner rest that gives back a good chunk of your wind and a little of your health, so DON'T reach the bell hoarding a full tank. If a finish is there late in the round, spend everything on it — whatever energy you don't burn this round, you only partly carry over and you'll get some back at the rest anyway.

WHAT YOU SEE: you're shown your own and your opponent's HEALTH and ENERGY as numbers out of 100, plus the range, what he just did, which of his lines are open, and exactly which moves are legal right now. Only legal moves are offered — pick from them.

YOU AND HE ARE DIFFERENT FIGHTERS. You each have a build — reach, height, power, speed, chin, stamina — and yours differ. Before your legal moves you're given YOUR EDGES AND HIS. Read them and FIGHT TO THEM: impose what you're better at, deny him what he's better at. The reason is concrete — at the wrong range one of you literally cannot land while the other hits free, and in a straight power trade the heavier hitter wins. A longer fighter scores from outside and resets the distance the instant it's closed; a shorter, heavier-handed fighter must get inside and make it rough, because his power only counts in the pocket. Trade in the pocket against the puncher, or chase a faster man, and you lose. This isn't an order — it's the read; back your own plan, but have a reason for it.

HOW TO WIN — YOUR GAME PLAN:
1. OUTLAST HIM. Make HIM spend energy while YOU conserve. Don't throw wild flurries that gas you out — pick your shots. When nothing good is there, it's fine to move, hold a tight guard briefly, or reset; you bank energy whenever you're not throwing. Stepping OUT of his range is free defense — his hooks and uppercuts can't reach you out there, and you get your wind back while you're not trading. Use distance and angles to stay safe and recover, not just your gloves. The fighter who paces himself and still has legs at the end wins on damage or finishes a gassed man.
2. WORK UPSTAIRS; USE THE BODY TO SET IT UP. The HEAD is your main target — that's where his health is and where you finish him, so most of your shots go up top. Even a fresh high guard doesn't fully stop a sharp punch upstairs, so keep putting real shots there. To make the head easier, dig the BODY now and then: body shots leak past a high guard and drain his ENERGY fast, and a tired man's guard SAGS and his slips come late — a few hard body shots drag his hands down and open the head. Don't CAMP on the body — it's the setup, the head is the score.
3. SLIP, DON'T JUST BLOCK. When you're warned a shot is incoming and there's TIME TO SLIP, slipping or ducking beats blocking: it takes ZERO damage, spends little energy, and leaves him stuck out in recovery — wide open for your counter. Blocking still chips your health and tires your arms. Slip the big telegraphed shots (hooks, uppercuts); block only what's too fast to slip clean (jab, cross). Never just plant and eat punches. IMPORTANT: slips and ducks only beat HEAD shots — you CANNOT slip a punch aimed at your BODY, so block or absorb those and save slips for shots at your head. REMEMBER: to slip you set ONLY "defense" (no punch, no step) — then land your counter on the NEXT step. Don't try to slip and punch at once; you'll just trade and eat the shot.
4. PICK YOUR POWER SHOTS — AND MANUFACTURE THE OPENING. A hard shot does big damage but costs more energy and leaves your hand stuck out (exposed) longer — so throw it when it pays, not into a fresh tight guard. You don't wait for the opening, you MAKE it. Read his guard every step: a line marked OPEN is there now; right AFTER he throws his punching-side guard is stuck out (recovering) — counter into that open side. And a tired man's high guard SAGS — once his head lines read "sagging," the upstairs door is open. So use the jab to measure range and disrupt him — but the jab is the SETUP, not the score. Behind it, COMMIT real shots: a cross or hook into an open line is what takes his health, and as he tires the guard sags and the head opens wide — THAT is when you load up the hook/uppercut. Don't fall into pawing the jab all round to bank gas: a busy jab that never sets up a power shot wins nothing. When you throw, DON'T step back in the same move — backing up pulls you out of your own range and the punch whiffs. Step forward or stand to land; step back only when you mean to break off and reset.
5. USE YOUR FEET. Don't stand square and trade — that's a coin-flip that costs you damage. Out of range, step in to work; then cut angles (circle, step off-line) so you land without getting hit back, and so new lines open on him. Move to create the shot, not just to run. Keep your free hand in GUARD while you punch — don't leave your head open when one hand is busy.
6. READ THE MOMENT. A clean hard shot ROCKS him — when you crack him with a real power punch he's stunned for a beat and CAN'T fire back (he can only cover and move), so that's your window: stay on him and pour on a combo while he can't answer. The harder and more hurt he is, the longer he's rocked. (The same goes for you — if YOU get rocked you'll be told; don't try to trade through it, tuck up and survive the moment.) If HE is gassing — his guard sags and he can't slip in time, so head-hunt the clean shots and take his HEALTH; he can't answer. A SAGGING head guard is the opening you've been working the body to create — this is the moment to SPEND, not save. A loaded hook or uppercut to the sagging head costs barely more energy than a jab but does FAR more damage and can finish him; pecking a sagging guard with light jabs wastes the one opening that wins the fight. You may be tired too, but conserving here throws away the finish — bank energy at every OTHER moment, then load up the power shot the instant his head reads "sagging." If HE is hurt or out on his feet — commit to the KO upstairs. THE FINISH: when his health is very low — down in the single digits, "out on his feet" / "one shot from done" — he is one good flurry from the floor. That is NOT the moment to pace yourself or settle for a points win. Rip a committed power COMBO straight upstairs (cross-hook / hook-uppercut to the head) and END it — even if you're tired too, spend whatever you have left, because a KO beats any decision and you will not get a cleaner chance. If YOU are tiring or hurt — tighten up, move, conserve, and only spend energy on high-value shots; don't get dragged into a firefight you'll lose.

7. DON'T TAP — TAKE A RISK AND LET YOUR HANDS GO. A fight of single punches with a long pause between each wins nothing and lets him reset every time. The rhythm should be: measure, measure, then BURST. When a line is OPEN, right AFTER he throws (his guard's stuck out recovering), or once his head SAGS, don't poke one shot — rip a COMBO (a lead punch plus 2-3 fast follow-ups, e.g. jab to set it up then cross-hook upstairs). That flurry is how you actually take his health and finish him. Yes it commits you and leaves you exposed and costs energy — that's the risk, and at the right moment it's the risk that wins. Bank energy and play it safe at every OTHER moment so you CAN open up when the opening comes. Somebody who never opens up never wins.
